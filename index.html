📄 Case Study

220 seats live in 47 days: how Corveta Health opened its first India GCC

Corveta Health had approval to build a 200-person analytics team in Chennai and a hiring plan that started in eight weeks. A conventional Grade-A lease would not have handed over a usable floor until the following year.

Intro

Corveta Health builds claims-analytics software for US payers. In late 2025 the board approved an India capability centre — engineering, data operations and clinical review under one roof — with a mandate to have the first 80 people productive inside a quarter.

The India leadership team had the headcount plan and the budget. What they did not have was time. Chennai's Grade-A market runs on nine-year leases with six-to-nine month fit-out cycles, security deposits measured in crores, and a vendor list — landlord, contractor, IT, facilities, security, cafeteria, transport — that lands on one person's desk on day one.

Full-width photo — client floor at the centre

Corveta Health's 220-seat floor at WorkEZ Willow Square, Guindy — handed over fitted, badged and network-ready.

Bridging gaps with solutions

Getting a floor before the lease cycle allowed

The challenge: Offers were already out to the first engineering cohort. A signed lease in January would still have meant an empty shell until August.

The solution: WorkEZ held 220 fitted seats at Willow Square, Guindy and ran the client-specific work — branding, cabin layout, a dedicated server room and separate badge zones — in parallel with contracting. The floor was handed over 47 days after signature, three weeks before the first joiners.

One accountable partner instead of nine vendors

The challenge: A two-person India setup team could not manage a landlord, a fit-out contractor, an ISP, a facilities agency, housekeeping, security, cafeteria and transport in their first quarter.

The solution: Everything above the desk stayed with WorkEZ — power and UPS backup, dual-ISP internet, IT support, housekeeping, pantry, security and visitor management — on a single monthly invoice with one named centre manager and a documented escalation path.

A floor that could pass a US security review

The challenge: Handling US health data meant the parent company's infosec team had to approve the physical site — access control, camera retention, visitor logs, network segregation and a lockable records room.

The solution: WorkEZ delivered card-controlled entry at both the floor and zone level, CCTV with agreed retention, a digital visitor trail exportable on request, a physically separate rack room and a VLAN isolated from the rest of the building. The infosec audit closed in a single site visit.

Designed for Chennai hiring, not just Chennai rent

The challenge: Corveta was competing for analytics talent against established GCCs. A candidate walking into a half-built floor was a lost offer.

The solution: Guindy sits on the metro line with the Anna Salai and OMR corridors both in reach, so commute was never the objection. The floor was interview-ready on day one — branded reception, finished meeting rooms, working cafeteria — and WorkEZ ran walk-in drives out of the 200-seater event space on three consecutive Saturdays.

Room to double without moving

The challenge: The three-year plan reaches 400 people. Outgrowing the floor in year two would have meant a second relocation and a second security review.

The solution: Corveta contracted 220 seats live with first right on a further 180 on the adjacent floor, drawn down in blocks of 40 on 60 days' notice. Expansion is a schedule change, not a new project.

“We were told a real office in Chennai takes nine months. We had 80 people working out of ours in seven weeks, and I never once had to chase a contractor. That is the whole reason we hit our hiring plan.”

👤
Priya Raghunathan
Head of India Operations, Corveta Health

Impact

Ten months in, the Chennai centre is Corveta's second-largest engineering site.

  • Hiring started two quarters early.
    The first cohort joined in February instead of August, pulling the product roadmap forward by roughly six months.
  • Zero capex on the office itself.
    No fit-out spend, no security deposit at lease scale. The India budget went to salaries and tooling.
  • One invoice, one escalation path.
    Facilities questions go to a named centre manager rather than eight vendor contacts, freeing the setup team for hiring and compliance.
  • Security cleared on the first visit.
    Access zoning, camera retention and network segregation were already in place, so the parent company's infosec review closed without a remediation list.
  • The next 180 seats are already reserved.
    Growth to 400 people happens on the same floor plate, under the same agreement, with no second relocation.

The results

Corveta Health did not set out to find a coworking space. It set out to open a capability centre on a timeline the Chennai lease market could not serve — and needed the office question to stop being a project so the team could get on with building one.

Choosing a managed floor converted a nine-month construction problem into a seven-week onboarding, removed eight vendor relationships from a two-person setup team, and left the expansion path pre-negotiated. The India leadership spent its first year on hiring and delivery rather than on switchgear and fire NOCs.

That is what a managed office is supposed to buy: not cheaper square feet, but the months you would otherwise spend getting to them.

Automatic lead notifications — Marketing & Pre-sales

New systemBackend + integrations
1The real problem to solve. This is not "send an email when a form submits" — that already half-exists and gets ignored. The requirement is that every lead reaches a named owner within minutes, with enough context to act, and nothing silently disappears. Three things make that work: one normalised lead event regardless of which form fired it, a routing rule that separates a brochure download from a 200-seat enterprise enquiry, and a delivery channel people actually watch. Email alone fails the third test.

Write to the CRM first. The record is the source of truth; notifications are a view of it.

Route

SLA: respond within 15 minutes.

Open in CRM
Subject line carries the qualification. Seats, company and centre in the subject means Pre-sales can triage from a phone lock screen. “New website enquiry” is why the current alerts get ignored.

Non-negotiables

Never lose a lead to a failed notification
Persist to the CRM before notifying. Queue notifications with retry and dead-letter handling. If Slack is down, the lead still exists and the digest still catches it.
Escalate on silence
Unclaimed past SLA → re-notify the channel, then the Pre-sales head at 2× SLA. A weekly “breached SLA” report goes to both team leads.
Deduplicate
Same email or phone within 24h → thread onto the existing record and post a reply in the thread, not a new alert. Otherwise a brochure + enquiry from one person reads as two leads.
Keep spam out of the channel
Honeypot, submit-timing check and rate limiting before the notification fires. A channel full of junk gets muted within a week, and then the system is worse than nothing.
Off-hours behaviour
Define working hours. Outside them, hot leads still post to the channel but suppress SMS; the SLA clock starts at the next working hour. Agree this with Pre-sales before build.
Don't leak PII
Notification channels must be private. No lead payload in public channels, GA4 events, or anywhere client-side. Consent timestamp and policy version stored with every record.
